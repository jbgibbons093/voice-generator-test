THE FULL STUDY

Out-of-Pocket Spending for Insulin by Medicare Beneficiaries After Monthly Caps

Hong and colleagues · JAMA Internal Medicine · 2026;186(6):723–731

Dongzhe Hong, Krystal Cantos, Diana Morgenstern, Sonali Dasgupta, Aaron S. Kesselheim, Zirui Song, Angela Y. Tong, Rohit Chadaram, Danielle Potter, Elisabetta Patorno, Jerry Avorn, and Benjamin N. Rome.

Read the paper ↗
Read the narration

We wanted to know whether making insulin more affordable could improve diabetes care, so we studied nearly five million Medicare beneficiaries with type two diabetes as the thirty-five-dollar monthly cap rolled out in twenty twenty-one and expanded in twenty twenty-three. After the expansion, insulin users paid about fifty-nine dollars less out of pocket per quarter and filled slightly more insulin. Among patients with lab results, average blood sugar also improved modestly, although serious low-blood-sugar events became slightly more common across the study population. Our findings suggest that lower costs can improve access, while persistent costs for other medicines point to the need for broader affordability policies. You can read our full study in JAMA Internal Medicine.
